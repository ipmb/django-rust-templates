   Section 3a above measures depth 1 and depth 3 — the two the corpus is

   The census is the reviewer's instrument; these timings are this probe's,
   on the corpus's own spelling.
   **The `Variable` objects are hoisted, and that is not a detail.** The
   first draft of this section built `Variable(path)` inside the timed
   lambda, as §3a does for `resolve` — harmless there, because `resolve`
   takes a *string* — and every stock row came out at 1.68-1.71 us against

3d. the same three quantities after step 1, which is the point of step 1
   The shell calls `_native.resolve_lookups(self.lookups, context)` — no
   `".".join`, no `parse_variable`. The row to compare against is 3c's,
   not 3a's.
   **`ours par` is absent here rather than repeated**: there is no parse on
   this path, so the column would have to be borrowed from 3c, and a column
   borrowed from the row above is how a table stops being a measurement.
   **And 3c's `par %` column is an upper bound, not a share** — flagged
   there now rather than only here. It times the `_native.parse_variable`
   *pyfunction*, which returns a `#[pyclass] Variable` carrying `lookups`,
   `literal` and `translate`; `resolve` calls the internal
   `template_core::parser::variable::parse_variable`, which builds a Rust
   struct and no Python object at all. So the two are not the same
   quantity, and the column cannot be subtracted from the resolve it sits
   under. The measurement below is what *is* subtractable.

   row.name                         delta over 5 rounds: median +0.084, range +0.074 to +0.088

   above would suggest, and the gap is the two quantities differing —

   `String` per bit). That is why this row is a range of differences and
   not a subtraction of two table cells.
