    workload    render us  walk-only us  machinery us   floor us  floor/walk

    large         184.253       178.793         5.460    172.933      0.9672
    `walk-only` is the same `NodeList.render` the floor replaces, with the per-call
    `push_state`/`bind_template` hoisted out, so `floor/walk` is the walk against the walk —
    no public-API machinery either side. It is a few percent below 1.0 at `mixed`/`large`
    and never a multiple: the floor deletes a Python loop over `Node`s and pays FFI crossings
    of comparable cost for what it still has to ask Python for. On a variable-heavy template
    that is most of the render, and the block below prices it.

  what the walk cannot reach -- the same tree with its look-ups made literals:
    workload            nodes   floor us  render us  floor med  render med
    154 look-ups          309    168.945    176.307    168.893     178.999
    154 literals          309    168.952     57.597    168.694      57.931
    `us` is best-of-7 per call; `med` is the median of the interleaved 30-round
    pairing. They disagree here because this effect is small against `measure()`'s noise,
    so every delta in the prose below is read from the `med` columns, not the `us` ones.
    The 154 `Context` look-ups cost stock 121.068 us (0.786 us each, from `render med`) and the floor 0.199 us
    (0.001 us each, from `floor med`). Per variable that is stock's 0.786
    against the floor's 0.001. `wins` counts rounds an arm's look-up came in under its
    literal one: stock's is 0/30, the floor's 12/30 and not stable across runs --
    the floor's delta is positive this run. So resolution is not where the floor's time goes, and
    the floor is not paying Django's Python `_resolve_lookup`.
    What is left is identical in both rows: one crossing to `render_value_in_context` per
    variable. Strip the look-ups and the floor spends 1.095 us per variable where stock spends
    0.376 us -- the same Python function, called from Rust instead of from Python.
    So the gate's few percent is the residue of two larger opposite effects: the floor wins at the
    resolution and hands most of it back at the escaper. On this evidence the headroom slice
    1 found is in *resolution*, and the escaper is what to bring across next -- not the
    dispatch, which the interleaved table below prices in its `tax% of render` column.

  engine).render(...)`: two independent totals, not addends of `parse` or of each other. Under

  beside ours, which is the comparison §3 exists to make.

    materialised render, and no column here is the gate.
