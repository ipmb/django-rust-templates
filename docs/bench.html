Every measurement

“spread” is worst/best across a row’s timed samples: a spread well above 1.00 means a noisy machine and a figure worth re-running. ≡ means the bytes matched the django control, ≠ that they did not, and — that the engine cannot render the row at all. “call” is that engine’s floor — the cost of a template that renders nothing, printed above — as a share of the cell, capped at 100% because the two are separate measurements: a row near call 100% is a measurement of the call, and a row near call 2% is a measurement of the construct. Read it down a column, not across a row: each cell divides by its own engine’s floor, and the floors are not equal — Jinja2’s is about twice stock Django’s — so two shares in one row have different numerators and are not comparable. Down a column the floor is fixed and the share answers one question: how much of this engine’s time on this row is the call.

Modes: render times a template that is already compiled; parse includes the compile, through from_string on all three engines so that none of them is served from a cache the others do not have. One asymmetry is inside these numbers and is named here rather than left for a reader to find: the Django-side engines are handed a Context built once per row rather than once per call, which is what the milestone table this supersedes measured — a view that builds one per request pays about the same again on both of them.
